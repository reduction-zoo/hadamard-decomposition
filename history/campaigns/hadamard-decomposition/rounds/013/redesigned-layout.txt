        NP-hardness of exact Hadamard decomposition
              with one rank bound equal to two

                      Working manuscript · Awaiting independent human expert review
                                            15 September 2026


Abstract
Exact Hadamard decomposition asks whether a rational matrix is the entrywise product of two real
matrices with prescribed rank bounds. We give a deterministic polynomial-time reduction from 3-SAT,
even when the input matrix is nonnegative integer and one rank bound is two. An identity submatrix
forces a basis of product vectors. Additional rows synchronize two-choice selectors, and weighted rows
around a cycle encode an exact subset-product equation. The converse holds for arbitrary real factors,
without sign restrictions. A determinant predicate recovers a satisfying assignment from every valid
decomposition. For witnesses encoded over a common real algebraic primitive element, a subresultant
and Sturm procedure implements recovery in polynomial bit time. The construction addresses an
explicitly stated complexity question; its novelty is supported within the literature coverage described
here and remains subject to expert assessment.

1. Introduction
Hadamard decomposition represents a matrix by multiplying low-rank matrices entrywise. It can
represent a matrix of rank equal to the product of the factor ranks. This expressive capacity motivates
its use as a multiplicative model for matrix compression. Ciaperoni, Gionis and Mannila introduced a
data-analysis formulation, placed exact real feasibility in the existential theory of the reals, and left its
formal computational complexity open [1].
Subsequent work developed alternating and manifold-based numerical algorithms [2], [3]. The product-
vector interpretation is also known: rank-one reshapes of row factors recover the Hadamard represen­
tation [3]. These algorithms and reformulations do not decide exact feasibility for all rational inputs. Our
contribution is a reduction that forces discrete choices within the unrestricted real factorization model.
For a rational matrix 𝑀 ∈ ℚ 𝜇×𝑁 and positive integer bounds 𝑝, 𝑞 ≤ min(𝜇, 𝑁 ), define exact Hadamard
feasibility by
                                          𝑀 = (𝑈 𝑉 ) ⊙ (𝑊 𝑍),                                            (1)

where 𝑈 ∈ ℝ𝜇×𝑝 , 𝑉 ∈ ℝ𝑝×𝑁 , 𝑊 ∈ ℝ𝜇×𝑞 and 𝑍 ∈ ℝ𝑞×𝑁 . The symbol ⊙ denotes entrywise multipli­
cation. Factor entries may have arbitrary signs; the two ordinary products have ranks at most their
respective bounds.
Theorem 1 (Main result). Exact Hadamard feasibility is NP-hard under deterministic polynomial-
time many-one reductions, even for nonnegative integer matrices with 𝑝 = 2. More precisely, a 3-SAT
formula with 𝑛 occurring variables and 𝑚 clauses maps to an instance with

                   𝐾 = max(3, 2𝑛 + 2𝑚),         𝜇 = 𝐾 2 + 9𝐾,      𝑁 = 4𝐾,     𝑞 = 2𝐾.                   (2)

The formula is satisfiable if and only if this instance is feasible. Every valid real decomposition deter­
mines a satisfying assignment by explicit determinant zero tests. On the common-primitive algebraic
witness encoding defined in Section 6, these tests give a deterministic algorithm polynomial in the
formula length plus the supplied witness length.
The source reduction uses the standard many-one framework [4]. A prime-exponent encoding first
expresses satisfaction as subset product. The new matrix construction then makes each item correspond
to one of two orientations of a product-vector selector. Calibration retains a nonzero continuous
parameter at each selector. A cycle of weighted rows makes the product of the selected items equal

4. Structure forced by a feasible decomposition
4.1. Saturation and pair sums
A nonzero tensor 𝑎 ⊗ 𝑏 is called a product vector. Under the usual coordinate identification, it is a rank-
one matrix. In a decomposition satisfying Equation 1, associate the product vector 𝑢𝐴 ⊗ 𝑤𝐴 to row
𝐴, and 𝑣𝐽 ⊗ 𝜁𝐽 to column 𝐽 . Their pairing is 𝑀𝐴,𝐽 . Here 𝑢𝐴 , 𝑤𝐴 are rows of 𝑈 , 𝑊 , while 𝑣𝐽 , 𝜁𝐽 are
columns of 𝑉 , 𝑍.
Lemma 2 (Saturation). The first 𝑁 = 4𝐾 row product vectors form a basis of ℝ2 ⊗ ℝ2𝐾 , and the
column product vectors form its dual basis. A later row with coefficients (𝑎𝐽 )𝐽 has product vector
∑𝐽 𝑎𝐽 𝑧𝐽 , where 𝑧𝐽 denotes the basis vector belonging to identity row 𝐽 .
Proof. The leading identity gives a nonsingular pairing matrix of size 𝑁 . The ambient tensor space
has dimension exactly 𝑁 . Both families are therefore bases and are dual. Pairing a later row with every
dual basis vector determines each of its coordinates. This forces its entire product vector, not just a
projection. Lower actual factor ranks cannot evade the argument, since the identity already requires
the full ambient dimension.                                                                           □
For two independent product vectors, their sum is a product vector exactly when they share a left or
a right factor line. If both pairs of factors were independent, linear functionals on their spans would
map the sum to a 2 × 2 identity matrix, contradicting rank one. The converse follows by distributing
the tensor product.

Three or more independent product vectors with product-vector pairwise sums must share one factor
line. To see this, fix two that share a left line and have distinct right lines. A third vector without that
left line would have to share both distinct right lines, which is impossible. Thus it has the common left
line. The argument with left and right exchanged is identical.

6.1. Recovery from arbitrary valid factors
The first row of 𝑈 belongs to identity index 𝐿0 . For each assignment item 𝑖, compute the determinant of
its left-factor rows at 𝐿0 and 𝑋𝑖 . By Lemma 3, this determinant is zero exactly when item 𝑖 is selected:

This predicate is invariant under invertible changes of left coordinates and nonzero row gauges. It

6.2. The encoded zero test
For executable recovery, encode all factor entries in one real algebraic primitive element 𝜃. The witness
supplies a nonzero integer polynomial 𝑃 and rational non-root endpoints 𝑎 < 𝑏 whose interval contains
exactly one distinct real root 𝜃 of 𝑃 . Each factor entry is a dense rational coordinate polynomial
evaluated at 𝜃. The polynomial 𝑃 may be reducible or have repeated roots. All degrees and coefficients
are written explicitly. Rational numerators and denominators are integer literals; compressed exponents
and symbolic expressions are excluded.
For the determinant polynomial 𝐷 in Equation 14, use the equivalence
                            𝐷(𝜃) = 0 ⇔ gcd(𝑃 , 𝐷) has a root in (𝑎, 𝑏).                             (15)

Every root of the gcd is a root of 𝑃 , so the isolating premise makes the right-hand root count zero or
one. A zero determinant gives gcd(𝑃 , 0) = 𝑃 ; a constant gcd has no root. This handles localization to
an irreducible factor without factoring 𝑃 or constructing another number field.
The executable computes the gcd up to scale as the last nonzero subresultant. For root counting, it
clears denominators, takes the primitive integer part, and removes repeated factors using the gcd with
the derivative. It then computes the sign-corrected subresultant Sturm sequence. Subtracting its sign-
variation count at 𝑏 from that at 𝑎, omitting zero evaluations, gives the required root count. These are
standard exact polynomial operations [5]. The implementation uses SymPy’s Poly.subresultants and
sturm_amv(method=1) the latter computes sign-corrected subresultants as described in Section 2.4.3 of
[6]. No feasibility solver runs inside either reduction map.

6.3. Bounds for both maps
Let ℓ be the source bit length and set 𝑆 = 𝑛 + 𝑚 + 2. The first 𝑛 + 𝑚 primes have magnitude
𝑂(𝑆 log 𝑆) by the classical explicit prime bounds [7]. Trial division generates them in polynomial time.
The total number of literal occurrences is 3𝑚, so each item, 𝑇 , and 𝑇 2 has 𝑂(𝑆 log 𝑆) bits. The dense
target consequently has

This is a loose encoding bound that charges the longest entry at every position. Schoolbook arithmetic
and serialization give conservative bounds

The rational witnesses in Lemma 4 also have polynomial encoding length: every ℎ𝑖 is an item subprod­
uct, with at most 𝑂(𝑆 2 log 𝑆) bits under a loose bound. This statement concerns the constructed YES
family only.
Let 𝐻 be the total serialized algebraic witness length and put 𝐵 = 𝐻 + 2. Literal rational parsing and
dense coordinate encoding ensure that degrees and input coefficient heights are at most 𝐵. Each deter­
minant has degree 𝑂(𝐵) and, after clearing denominators, coefficient height 𝑂(𝐵2 ). A subresultant
coefficient is a determinant of a Sylvester submatrix of order 𝑂(𝐵), giving height 𝑂(𝐵3 ) for the
normalized gcd. The square-free part retains this polynomial height; its sign-corrected subresultants
have height 𝑂(𝐵4 ). The subresultant size bounds underlying these steps are standard; see Theorems
2.1–2.4 of [5].
The controlled remainder procedure has 𝑂(𝐵3 ) rational coefficient operations per sequence. Allowing
𝑂(𝐵8 ) bits per intermediate numerator or denominator conservatively covers polynomial divisions
on these bounded-degree, bounded-height inputs. Schoolbook rational arithmetic, normalization and
endpoint evaluation therefore fit within 𝑂(𝐵24 ) bit operations per determinant. This deliberately loose
bound applies to the subresultant procedure actually called. With 2𝑛 determinant queries and one field
check, recovery takes

The converse in Lemma 4 and the predicate in Equation 14 hold over all real factors. The executable
guarantee concerns the finite algebraic encoding just defined. Since the feasibility equations have
rational coefficients, a nonempty real solution set has a real-algebraic point by real closed field transfer.
This fact does not assert polynomial witness length for the unrestricted problem.
Malformed source encodings map outside the target language. The canonical occurring-variable repre­
sentation is polynomially obtainable from ordinary 3-SAT. Together, the construction, equivalence,
recovery and bounds prove Theorem 1.

7. Relation to previous work and limits
The target is the factor-width formulation of Ciaperoni, Gionis and Mannila, Equation (4) of [1], with
exact feasibility discussed in their Section 4.3 and the complexity question in Section 9. Our input
matrices are integer and nonnegative; all factor variables remain unrestricted real. This distinction
matters because imposing extra factor conditions changes the decision problem.
Identity factorizations and tensor-product representations are prior ingredients. Wertz, Vandaele and
Gillis give an identity construction in Appendix B, Lemma 2 of [2]. Gillis and coauthors give a face-
splitting and rank-one reshape formulation in Lemmas 2.3–2.4 of [3]. The contribution here is the
synchronization of all selector blocks, calibration of their residual scales, and the multiplicative cycle.
Low-rank matrix signing and entrywise power factorization have their own hardness results [8]. In
particular, Theorem 2.2 of that paper establishes hardness for prescribed magnitudes of a single factor.
Two independent Hadamard factors admit additional magnitude freedom, so that theorem alone does
not establish the present result. Generic identifiability of Hadamard–Hitchcock tensor decompositions
[9] likewise does not decide exact feasibility of every matrix.
Primary-literature searches through 15 September 2026 found no matching hardness theorem for this
unrestricted real two-factor model. Coverage included the papers above, Hadamard factorization aliases,
subset product, product bases and generic Hadamard-rank results. Unindexed manuscripts, exhaustive
citation closure and overlooked implications of general representation theorems remain outside that
coverage. The claimed new construction requires independent human assessment of correctness and
novelty.

8. Conclusion
Synchronized product-vector selectors convert exact subset product into Hadamard feasibility at widths
2 and 2𝐾. Their cycle gives a Karp reduction from 3-SAT, and their left-factor directions provide witness
recovery for arbitrary valid factors. The result identifies a hardness boundary with one rank bound
fixed. It does not establish strong NP-hardness, approximation hardness, membership of the unrestricted
problem in NP, or completeness for the existential theory of the reals.

A. Verification and reproducibility
A.1. Evidence and its scope
The implementation was checked with Python 3.14.7, SymPy 1.14.0 and Z3 5.1.0. The manuscript uses Typst
0.15.1. The prepared suite tests six source formulas and performs eight recovery calls. A separately imple­
mented verifier tests 29 distinct formulas, including 27 YES and two NO instances, and performs 109 recovery
calls. Nine of these calls use algebraic gauges, including negative roots, cubic roots and reducible defining
polynomials. Four focused calls reuse an existing source formula; they are not extra distinct instances. The
generated cases use seed 13013 and two through four occurring variables. The largest tested target has 682
rows and 88 columns, with widths 2 and 44.
The verifier runs the actual candidate in fresh processes. It enumerates source assignments and validates
the actual matrix against the constructed family before solving its subset-product coefficients by exact
meet-in-the-middle enumeration. It independently builds target factors, checks every matrix entry, invokes
recovery, and checks the returned assignment directly. Variants include non-diagonal coordinate changes,
right-coordinate mixing, and signed row and column gauges.
The prepared target oracle instead uses Z3 Boolean-controlled linear prefix equations. Its 32 standalone three-
item checks agree with exhaustive subset enumeration, with 16 YES and 16 NO cases. A separate 38-case
arithmetic check compares sign-corrected subresultant root counts with a rational Sturm implementation.
Scientific notation is rejected before coefficient or endpoint conversion. The defining quartic real-factor
oracle is retained for tiny target examples; an unknown outcome is an error.
Both structural target NO oracles depend on Lemmas 2–4. Their finite agreement cannot establish those
lemmas. A separate reviewer inspected the general proof and constructed further checks without importing
the candidate. These included an unrestricted local real escape query, exact symbolic cycle identities, and full
witnesses using repeated, reducible defining polynomials and higher-degree coordinate perturbations. The
final review found no remaining repair requirement. This agent review and the finite checks do not replace
independent human expert verification.

A.2. Files and commands
All paths below are relative to the repository root. The candidate and contract are in campaigns/
hadamard-decomposition/work/. The files algorithm.py, proof.md, contract.md, preparation.md and
verification.md describe the rule and its checks. Current raw outputs are in campaigns/hadamard-
decomposition/rounds/013/: literal-rational-prepared-check.txt, literal-rational-check.txt,
structural-oracle-check.txt and arithmetic-check.txt. The final review is campaigns/hadamard-
decomposition/reviews/literal-rational/review.md. Earlier evidence remains with its owning round or
review.
From the repository root, run:

  cd campaigns/hadamard-decomposition/work
  python3 check.py --self-test
  python3 check.py --oracle product-basis --candidate algorithm.py
  python3 verify.py --candidate algorithm.py
  typst compile manuscript.typ manuscript.pdf


The two candidate suites execute both maps. There are no configured solver, subprocess or campaign
timeouts. The finite input domains are declared in cases.json and verify.py. To run the maps on supplied
inputs:

  python3 algorithm.py < source.json > target.json
  python3 algorithm.py --extract < request.json > assignment.json


Here source.json is a formula with keys n and clauses. The second input has keys source and
target_solution. Its target solution uses the four factor matrices plus field, exactly as specified in
contract.md. Each coordinate is a dense list of literal rational coefficients. These two command modes share
no process state.
